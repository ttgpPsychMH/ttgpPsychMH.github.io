My research interests lie at the intersection of psychology, mental health, and organizational research. I am particularly interested in understanding psychological well-being in work and educational settings, with an emphasis on quantitative and applied research.

## Research areas

### Occupational mental health and employee well-being

My work in this area focuses on psychological experiences in the workplace, including work engagement, burnout, psychological distress, stress, and employee well-being.

### Psychological assessment and quantitative research

I am interested in the measurement of psychological constructs and in the use of quantitative methods to examine relationships among individual, occupational, and organizational factors.

### Mental health across populations

Beyond workplace research, I have contributed to studies involving students, young adults, parents, and other populations, with topics including stress, anxiety, depression, stigma, social support, gratitude, religiosity, and quality of life.

## Methods and analytical approaches

My research experience includes study design, data collection, manuscript preparation, and quantitative analysis. Methods and tools I have worked with include:

- Descriptive and inferential statistics
- Group comparison tests
- Correlation and regression analysis
- Exploratory factor analysis
- Confirmatory factor analysis
- Structural equation modeling
- Partial least squares structural equation modeling
- Bibliometric analysis

Software used in my research includes SPSS, AMOS, JASP, SmartPLS, and R.

## Current direction

My current work continues to develop around occupational mental health, organizational behavior, psychological assessment, and quantitative research methodology. This page will be updated as ongoing projects and new research outputs are added.
